Each position is a real bandbox of London or Scottish Control, with its own airspace, levels and neighbours. The AI controllers work the sectors round you and hand traffic to you at the boundaries.

Session

London Control South

  • Flights coming your way are offered to you by the unit next door: accept them (ACC, or click the strip) before they reach your boundary. They call you about a minute out.
  • Answer each check-in (ID or any instruction). Climb departures to their cruising level, descend arrivals to the level the standing agreement gives for the next sector, and transfer each flight (XFR) before it leaves your airspace.
  • Keep 5 NM or 1,000 ft between your flights and everything else. When an airport is holding the terminal unit only takes one arrival per slot: hold the rest at a fix of yours (HOLD SFD) or slow them down (M76).
  • Commands: BAW123 C350 climb, D240 descend, H270 heading, DCT SFD, RES own navigation, M78 / S280 speed, HOLD SFD, XFR, ACC, DIV EGKK.

How it works

Same console, bigger map.

The street map, the buttons, the strips and the command line are the ones you know from the airports. The difference is the job: you own a block of airspace, not an airport.

  1. Accept and identifyInbound flights appear on your strips as offers from Paris, Brest, Shannon, Maastricht, the terminal sectors or the next London sector. Accept them, then answer their call when they come on frequency.
  2. Climb, descend, separateDepartures want their cruising level, arrivals want to come down. Keep everyone 5 NM or 1,000 ft apart: the short-term conflict alert warns you two minutes ahead.
  3. Work the agreementsEach pair of sectors has a standing agreement: the level a flight crosses the boundary at for its destination. The next unit refuses a flight that isn't descending to it.
  4. Delays, weather and the unexpectedAirports back up and hold, close and send traffic elsewhere. Neighbours regulate the flow. Thunderstorms build and crews ask to go round. And sometimes a crew declares an emergency.
Sources and accuracy

What is real and what is modelled.

Sector boundaries, levels and positions, airways, fixes, navaids, SIDs, STARs and the standing agreements between sectors come from the VATSIM UK sector file, which follows the UK AIP (ENR 2, ENR 3, ENR 4 and AD 2) with the sector split VATSIM UK uses; real NATS sectorisation and frequencies differ in places. Planned traffic comes from the Clearway airline timetables of Heathrow, Gatwick, Manchester, Bristol and London City and from typical flows over the UK. Live traffic comes from the adsb.lol and adsb.fi networks through the site’s relay; once a flight is yours it follows your instructions. Aircraft performance and the upper wind are modelled. This is a training game, not for real-world navigation, and is not affiliated with NATS or any airline.
